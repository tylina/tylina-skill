# Style Anchor: data-viz

Information-first visual style focused on charts, diagrams, and data structures. Minimal decoration, clear axes, generous labeling space, and restrained color used only to encode meaning.

---

## 1. Visual Characteristics

- Clear structural hierarchy: axes, gridlines, data marks, annotations
- Generous white margins and internal padding (20%+ breathing room)
- Restrained palette where color encodes data categories, not decoration
- Thin precise lines (1-2pt) for structure; thicker marks (4-6pt) for data
- No ornamental elements — every mark carries information

---

## 2. Best-For Scenarios

- Management consulting and strategy decks
- Research findings and scientific presentations
- Financial analysis and investor reports
- Comparative analysis and benchmarking decks
- Any deck where the data IS the story

---

## 3. Prompt Fragment (paste as opening sentences)

> Data visualization illustration with information-first composition. Thin precise structural lines (1pt axes, 0.5pt gridlines), bold data marks (4-6pt), generous white margins at 20% on all sides. Color used only to distinguish data categories — never decorative. Every element serves an information purpose.

---

## 4. Color Behavior

- Primary HEX encodes the main data series or most important category
- Secondary HEX fills the background or represents the baseline/reference
- Accent HEX highlights one anomaly, callout, or key insight point
- Additional data categories use 40% and 70% opacity variants of primary — not new hues

---

## 5. Don'ts

- No 3D effects on charts (perspective distorts data perception)
- No decorative illustrations alongside data (icons, cartoons, mascots)
- No dense color fills behind chart areas (the background must stay neutral)
- No more than 5 data categories in a single view (split into multiple images)
- No implied narrative through visual drama — let the data structure speak

---
