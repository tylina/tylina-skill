# Style Anchor: watercolor

Soft pigment edges with visible paper bleed, warm organic tones, and a hand-painted feel. Shapes are loose and suggestive rather than precise — the medium's natural unpredictability is a feature.

---

## 1. Visual Characteristics

- Soft irregular edges where pigment bleeds into wet paper
- Visible paper texture (cold-press watercolor paper grain)
- Color pooling and concentration variations within single washes
- Warm undertones from the paper showing through transparent pigment layers
- Intentional white (unpainted) areas as compositional breathing space

---

## 2. Best-For Scenarios

- Cultural and arts-focused presentations
- Wellness, mindfulness, and lifestyle decks
- Storytelling and narrative-driven content
- Travel and place-based visual essays
- Any deck where warmth and organic humanity build emotional connection

---

## 3. Prompt Fragment (paste as opening sentences)

> Watercolor painting on cold-press textured paper. Soft pigment edges bleeding into the paper surface, visible color pooling and concentration variations within each wash. Transparent layers allow warm paper tone to show through. Intentional unpainted white areas provide compositional breathing space.

---

## 4. Color Behavior

- Primary HEX appears as the dominant wash (the largest pigment area), slightly desaturated by paper transparency
- Secondary HEX manifests as a lighter background wash or the paper-tint showing through
- Accent HEX is a concentrated pigment drop — small, intense, wet-on-wet blooming outward
- All colors appear more transparent and warm-shifted than their HEX values suggest (watercolor convention)

---

## 5. Don'ts

- No hard geometric edges or ruler-straight lines (breaks the organic medium)
- No solid opaque fills — every area must show paper texture through the pigment
- No digital-perfect symmetry (watercolor is inherently asymmetric and organic)
- No dark heavy backgrounds (watercolor works light-to-dark, paper is always the lightest value)
- No more than 3-4 pigment colors per composition (overworked watercolor looks muddy)

---

## 6. Example Prompt

> Watercolor painting on cold-press textured paper. Soft pigment edges bleeding into the paper surface, visible color pooling and concentration variations within each wash. Transparent layers allow warm paper tone to show through. Intentional unpainted white areas provide compositional breathing space. A loose botanical arrangement — three abstract flower forms clustered in the center-right of the frame, each rendered as overlapping circular washes with darker pigment concentration at petal centers. Stems are single confident brushstrokes descending to the lower edge. The left 40% of the canvas is unpainted white paper, creating breathing room. Flowers are washed in soft rose (#E11D48 at 40% pigment density), leaves and stems in sage green (#4D7C0F at 50% density), and one small concentrated bloom in deep plum (#7C2D12 at full concentration). Cold-press paper grain visible throughout, especially in lighter wash areas. Aspect ratio 3:2, landscape. No text, no labels, no borders.
